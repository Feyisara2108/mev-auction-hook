Sustainable Liquidity & MEV Protection
The Problem

Liquidity providers are quietly losing money

Every day, trading bots exploit Uniswap pools. They trade against slightly stale prices, take the profit, and leave the loss with the people who provided the liquidity.

01

Bots win

Arbitrageurs and MEV bots race to trade against out-of-date prices and extract the difference.

02

LPs eat the loss

That extracted value comes straight out of the liquidity providers' pockets. Fees don't fully cover it.

03

The money leaves

The profit exits the protocol entirely — it goes to bots and validators, and never comes back.

Why it happens

A pool can't tell a bot from a normal person

A Uniswap pool has one fee for everyone and no way to tell an arbitrage bot apart from an ordinary trader. So it faces a bad choice:

Raise fees for everyone

Punishes ordinary traders for damage they never caused — and pushes volume to other exchanges.

Do nothing

Let the bots keep winning, and let liquidity providers keep absorbing the loss. This is the status quo today.

There has never been a third option — until now.

Our Solution

Auction the MEV — and keep it in the pool

Instead of letting bots quietly extract that value, we turn every large swap into a tiny, few-second on-chain auction. Bots now have to bid for the right to execute the trade — and that bid money stays in the pool instead of leaving it.

How it works · in plain English

One trade becomes three simple steps

1 · Request
You submit your swap. Small swaps go through instantly. Large swaps open a short bidding window (about 3 seconds).
→
2 · Bid
MEV searchers compete during the window, each bidding higher for the right to execute your swap. Highest bid wins.
→
3 · Execute
The swap runs. The winning bid is split — part goes back to LPs, part is rebated to you, the trader.

No bids? The swap still goes through normally. A trade can never be blocked.

The new part

The liquidity providers run the economics

The split of that recaptured money isn't fixed by us. Each pool's LPs vote on it — and your vote counts in proportion to how much liquidity you've actually provided. Bigger stake, bigger say.

60% → Liquidity Providers
40% → Trader rebate

Example split from a real on-chain vote. Default is 100% to LPs — a pool chasing volume can vote to hand traders a rebate and pull trade away from other exchanges. It's their call.

What makes it different

Everyone else needs off-chain machinery. We don't.

Other MEV-protection systems rely on off-chain relayers, solvers, or operators to run the auction. Ours runs entirely on-chain — no middlemen, no servers, nothing to trust.
